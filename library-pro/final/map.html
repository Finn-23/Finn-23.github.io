Each floor of any library presents something more than just a random arrangement of desks and shelves. A library's floor plan may at first appear to be rather mundane and uninteresting, a closer and more thorough observation reveals it to be a lively and changing space. Spending a few hours within a library can expose patterns of behavior shaped by the subtle differences in microhabitats formed by the layout of desks, shelves, windows, and walls. Certain niches are consistently filled and the locations are hard to find without energy drinks and backpacks claiming them, while other spaces seem perpetually empty and layered with dust.

Below, we have created a copy of the third floor layout of the Library of Science and Medicine (see our personal, boots-on-the-ground review of the space). We have chosen to highlight a number of significant microhabits that help illustrate how diverse the library floor space actually is.

Stairwell

This brick-walled stairwell is a space that serves as the primary way to access the third floor of the Library of Science and Medicine. It is a conduit for migration between the different study environments provided by each floor. Actions within this space are typically brief and directional, though periods of congestion can occur during peak class transitions, creating temporary clustering near landings. Students are also often observed stepping away from their studies and into this space to take phone calls.

Study Room

This is one of three collaborative enclosures located on the floor. It is a habitat that supports highly social and vocal behavioral patterns that are rarely tolerated in surrounding ecosystems. Small groups use the enclosed environment to exchange information, coordinate tasks, and engage in collective problem-solving activities without disrupting nearby populations. Occupancy levels fluctuate throughout the day, though competition for access intensifies during examination periods.

Back to Map

Solitary Nook

This cozy nook is tucked in a secluded corner of the floor, creating a low-disturbance microhabitat that is favored by students seeking near-total separation from external activity. Shielding from foot traffic and conversation, provided by the surrounding walls and rows of library shelves creates an unusually stable environment, allowing students to maintain prolonged periods of uninterrupted focus. This is another space that becomes highly sought after during periods of elevated academic stress, like midterms and finals week. Individuals occupying the nook often display long-duration residency patterns and minimal social interaction, emerging only intermittently to gather coffee and chips before returning to isolation.

Naturally Lit Group Study Space

This semi-social habitat allows individuals to remain spatially connected with nearby peers while maintaining the relatively subdued atmosphere of the surrounding floor. Large windows provide consistent natural illumination, creating a calmer environmental condition. The natural light helps students maintain natural circadian rhythms, promoting a more natural sleep schedule. Unlike fully enclosed collaborative zones, interaction within this habitat is generally indirect and low-volume. Occupants often work independently while benefiting from the subtle social presence of neighboring individuals, forming a balanced ecosystem between isolation and collective activity. Competition for seating increases during daylight hours, when the habitat’s lighting conditions are at their most favorable.

Solo Study Desk

This habitat supports sustained periods of independent academic activity within a low-noise environment, much like the nearby solitary nook. The semi-enclosed desk structures create a modest territorial boundary, reducing visual distractions and encouraging concentrated, task-oriented behavior. Occupants typically establish temporary residency for extended intervals, often surrounding themselves with study materials and coffee cups to reinforce control over the space. While less isolated than deeper refuge habitats, the workstation maintains a stable and predictable atmosphere favored by individuals seeking quiet, uninterrupted productivity.

Artifically Lit Group Space

This habitat provides a more controlled environmental condition for individuals who prefer minimal exposure to external light cycles. This space serves as an alternative to the naturally lit group study area with consistent artificial illumination createing a stable atmosphere largely unaffected by weather or time of day. The space supports quiet forms of collective study, with occupants maintaining close spatial proximity while limiting disruptive interaction.

Library Shelves

While itself not a traditional study habitat, these shelves occupying a significant portion of the floor’s terrain. The rows and rows of gray, metal shelves function as both resource archive and structural habitat divider. Their primary role is the long-term storage of scientific journals and reference texts, but their physical arrangement plays a critical ecological function by shaping movement pathways and fragmenting open space into smaller, semi-isolated study zones. The labyrinthine structure of the shelving rows dampens sound transmission and reduces visual exposure between adjacent habitats, creating pockets of relative privacy for nearby study sites. These formations indirectly support a wide range of behavioral patterns by stabilizing surrounding micro-environments, allowing both solitary and group study populations to coexist in close proximity with minimal disturbance.
